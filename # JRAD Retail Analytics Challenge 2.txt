# JRAD Retail Analytics Challenge 2026

> An end-to-end retail analytics project built from scratch and inspired by real-life observations of Nigerian supermarket operations.

## About the Project

JRAD Retail Analytics Challenge 2026 is a simulated multi-branch retail analytics project designed to give aspiring Data Analysts, Analytics Engineers, Data Engineers, and SQL learners a realistic relational dataset for hands-on practice.

Unlike a typical single-file dataset, this project contains multiple interconnected datasets representing different areas of retail operations:

- Sales transactions
- Receipts
- Payments
- Customers
- Products
- Pricing history
- Inventory
- Employees and cashiers
- Branch behaviour
- Monthly shopping behaviour
- Business and seasonal events

The project was independently designed and developed by **Sodiq Basit Oluwatimileyin**.

It was inspired by observations of Nigerian supermarket operations and translated into a complete simulated data environment using Excel, Python, SQL, and data-modelling techniques.

---

# Project Objective

The goal of this challenge is to allow participants to practice working with a realistic retail database and answer meaningful business questions using SQL.

JRAD is a simulated multi-branch Nigerian retail business operating across five locations.

Management wants to better understand:

- What drives revenue?
- Which products and categories perform best?
- Which branches generate the highest sales?
- How do registered and walk-in customers behave differently?
- Which payment methods are most frequently used?
- Which products face inventory risks?
- How do pricing changes affect the business?
- Which employees and cashiers process the highest transaction volumes?
- How do branch behaviour and business events influence sales?

# Part 1: Data Quality Assessment

Before performing business analysis, investigate the quality and structure of the data.

Suggested tasks:

1. List all available tables.
2. Count the number of records in each dataset.
3. Check for duplicate records.
4. Identify missing values in important columns.
5. Validate Product_ID references.
6. Validate Customer_ID references.
7. Validate Employee_ID and Cashier_ID references.
8. Check for negative quantities or invalid sales values.
9. Reconcile payment totals with receipt or sales totals where appropriate.
10. Determine the overall date range of the dataset.

# Part 2: SQL Business Questions

## A. Sales Performance

1. What is the total revenue generated?
2. How many unique receipts were generated?
3. How many transaction line items were recorded?
4. What is the average transaction value?
5. Which branch generated the highest revenue?
6. Rank branches by revenue, receipts and quantity sold.
7. Which branch has the highest average transaction value?
8. Which month generated the highest revenue?
9. Which dates generated the highest number of receipts?
10. Analyse revenue trends over time.

## B. Product Performance

11. What are the top 10 products by revenue?
12. What are the top 10 products by quantity sold?
13. Which categories generate the highest revenue?
14. Which departments perform best?
15. Which products generate high revenue despite relatively low sales volume?
16. Which products generate high sales volume but relatively low revenue?
17. Which products perform best in specific branches?
18. Which products are sold across the highest number of branches?
19. Which popularity tiers generate the strongest performance?

## C. Customer Analysis

20. How many registered and walk-in customers are represented?
21. What percentage of revenue comes from registered versus walk-in customers?
22. Compare the average transaction value of registered and walk-in customers.
23. Who are the top customers by total spending?
24. Who are the top customers by number of transactions?
25. How does loyalty membership relate to customer spending?
26. Which acquisition sources produce the highest-value customers?
27. Which customer personas are associated with higher spending?
28. Which shopping missions generate the largest baskets?

## D. Payment Analysis

29. Which payment method is used most frequently?
30. What percentage of revenue comes from Transfer, POS and Cash?
31. Which payment method has the highest average transaction value?
32. Which branches rely most heavily on each payment method?
33. Analyse payment method usage over time.
34. Investigate unsuccessful or unusual payment records.

## E. Cashier and Employee Analysis

35. Which cashiers processed the highest number of receipts?
36. Which cashiers processed the highest transaction value?
37. Which branches have the highest cashier workload?
38. Which cashiers handle the highest average transaction value?
39. Compare transaction activity with employee performance ratings where relevant.
40. Compare revenue per cashier across branches.

## F. Inventory Analysis

41. How many products are In Stock, Low Stock and Out of Stock?
42. Which branches have the highest number of out-of-stock products?
43. Which products have high sales but low remaining inventory?
44. Identify products at risk of stockout.
45. Which products have high opening stock but relatively low sales?
46. Which branches have the highest total current stock?

## G. Pricing and Inflation Analysis

47. How many products experienced price changes?
48. Which products experienced the highest percentage price increase?
49. Which categories experienced the highest average price increase?
50. What are the most common reasons for price changes?
51. Compare sales performance around pricing changes where possible.
52. Do highly inflation-sensitive products experience more frequent price changes?

## H. Branch Behaviour and Events

53. Does actual sales performance align with the branch behaviour assumptions?
54. Do branches with stronger bread demand actually sell more bread?
55. Do premium-demand branches generate more premium product revenue?
56. Do family-shopping branches generate larger baskets?
57. How do business events affect revenue, receipts and basket behaviour?
58. Compare performance before, during and after selected events.
59. Did targeted branches or categories benefit during relevant events?
